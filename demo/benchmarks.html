SDG 11.6.2 · undata/sdg/EN_ATM_PM25

Fine particulate matter

Tier 1 · city aggregate Proxy

Where NYC sits

6.60 µg/m³ in 2019, against 7.57 for the US city aggregate — and the gap has widened every year since 2016.

Why this one is Tier 1

The UN publishes a degree-of-urbanisation slice, so this compares NYC to the city parts of the US rather than to the country. Only five indicators have it.

Read with this

Units agree (µg/m³) and both are annual means, but the methods differ: NYC's figure comes from NYCCAS ground monitors, the UN's is a population-weighted modelled estimate. Comparable in level, not interchangeable.

SDG 16.1.1 · undata/sdg/VC_IHR_PSRC

Intentional homicide

Tier 3 · national Proxy

The crossing

NYC ran above the national rate until 2013, then below it — and stayed below through the 2021 spike. Invisible from either dataset alone.

The 2020 gap is deliberate

There is no ACS 1-year release for 2020, so there is no denominator and no rate — on the year NYC's count jumped to 456. We left it empty.

Read with this

NYPD records murder & non-negligent manslaughter, which tracks the UNODC intentional-homicide definition closely. NYC publishes a count, not a rate — the denominator is Census ACS annual population. Pre-2006 rows in the NYPD file carry junk incident dates (17 murders in 1990) and are excluded.

SDG 11.6.1 · undata/sdg/EN_MWT_RCYR

Municipal waste recycled

Tier 3 · national Proxy

The naive read

NYC recycles 17.2% against 23.6% for the US in 2018 — a six-point gap, and an easy headline.

The honest read

The two numbers do not cover the same waste. The gap is real but overstated, and by an amount nobody has quantified.

This is the chart the whole project exists for

DSNY tonnage covers residential collection only. The UN municipal-waste definition includes commercial waste. Same units, same period, same apparent shape — and the comparison is still not apples to apples. Every automated check passes here. Only a person reading both definitions catches it.

The eight that don't — and why

A blocked pair is not a failure of the pipeline. Recording why two numbers cannot share an axis is the product; a crosswalk showing only the easy pairs would be the thing we are building against.

IndicatorVerdictWhy it fails
Road traffic deathsSDG 3.6.1 Blocked NYC has daily Vision Zero data, every fatality geocoded. The UN has one observation — a 2021 modelled estimate. Blocked by the UN side.
Maternal mortalitySDG 3.1.1 Blocked The UN rate is per 100,000 live births, not per population — the denominator lives in a different NYC dataset. NYC also publishes pregnancy-associated deaths, a deliberately broader definition.
Child mortalitySDG 3.2.1 Context only UN counts deaths under five; NYC's series is under one. Both are raw counts, so the units "agree" — which is exactly the trap. The chart would look perfect and compare different populations.
PovertySDG 1.2.1 Context only The UN series is the international extreme-poverty line (~$2.15/day), near zero for any US city. NYC's own measure is an order of magnitude higher. Two numbers sharing a name and measuring nothing alike.
Deaths from air pollutionSDG 3.9.1 Context only UN reports a national death count from ambient air pollution; NYC reports an age-adjusted rate for ages 30+ from PM2.5 specifically. Different measure, age base and pollutant scope.
Inadequate housingSDG 11.1.1 Context only UN measures the share of urban population in slums. NYC's nearest analogue is a count of hazardous housing violations — an enforcement output that tracks inspection rates as much as conditions.
Built-up area per capitaSDG 11.7.1 No NYC source Tier 1 UN data with city-level slices across countries, 2000–2025. No NYC equivalent identified yet. Recorded so the gap stays visible rather than forgotten.
Safely managed drinking waterSDG 6.1.1 No signal Sits at ~100% for the US across the whole window, as it would for NYC. Two flat lines at 100 tell an analyst nothing. Excluded deliberately — a ceiling effect is a reason not to build a view, and that judgment belongs written down.

The inversion

We expected the UN to be the rich side and NYC the patchy one. On the city's flagship street-safety programme, it is the other way round.

New York City · Vision Zero
Every fatality, geocoded, daily Motor Vehicle Collisions — Crashes, ~2.2M records since 2012, updated continuously.
UN · SDG 3.6.1
One number, 2021 A single modelled WHO estimate for the United States. No trend is possible.

For road safety, the international comparison a city would most want simply does not exist. That is not a gap in NYC's reporting — it is an argument for city data flowing toward the UN system, not only the other way.

Method. 689 base indicators enumerated from the SDG goal trees via the UN Data Commons MCP and REST endpoints; 248 screened as having a usable US series; NYC counterparts proposed by embedding search over all 2,400 NYC Open Data datasets, then graded by hand. Comparability grades are human judgments — the harness verifies that a mapping still resolves and that units agree, and a human grade vetoes it.

Every number here is re-derivable. Code, crosswalk and full working notes: github.com/sarapis/undatacommons-nyc · project briefing. Figures retrieved 14 Sep 2026 against the UN Data Commons staging deployment; the platform goes public 17 Sep 2026.
